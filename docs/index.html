Estimating flood inundation probability in space

Each 500-meter grid cell receives a prediction interpreted as the probability that the area will be inundated with flood water. The model is trained and validated on Calgary, Alberta, then used to predict for two comparable US cities chosen on a terrain gradient. The transfer tests what the model learned and where its validity ends.

DataCalgary

The label is the 1-in-100-year flood extent

Calgary is divided into a fishnet of 500-meter cells. A cell is labeled inundated when more than five percent of its area falls inside the city’s official 1% annual-chance flood extent, a modeled steady-state hazard rather than a single storm.

Feature 1 of 4Calgary

Distance to river

Distance from each cell centroid to the river network. Inundated cells cluster within a few hundred meters of a channel, which makes distance the dominant predictor. Darker cells are closer.

Feature 2 of 4Calgary

Slope

Mean terrain slope from a satellite elevation model. Slope runs slightly higher in flooded cells because channels sit below valley walls, so it partly marks valley terrain rather than causing flooding. The next feature separates the two.

Feature 3 of 4Calgary

Elevation

Water accumulates at low elevation. Elevation and slope jointly distinguish valley floors, which flood, from valley walls, which do not.

Feature 4 of 4Calgary

Impervious share

The share of each cell covered by pavement and rooftops, from the city’s landcover composite. Impervious surfaces convert rainfall to runoff rather than infiltration, raising flood odds where terrain concentrates the flow.

ModelCalgary

Four features, one logistic regression

Features are z-scored within the city, so every coefficient reads as the change in flood odds per standard deviation, and the coefficients are directly comparable. Distance and elevation lower the odds. Impervious share raises them.

Standardization also underwrites the later city transfer. Each city’s features are measured relative to that city, so different instruments measuring the same quantity land on the same scale.

70/30 train and test split, fixed seed. Full specification in the methods report.

ThresholdCalgary

Threshold selection under class imbalance

Nine percent of cells flood, so a conventional 0.5 cutoff buries the minority class. The threshold is instead tuned with Youden’s index, landing near 0.10. A missed flood cell costs more than a spurious flag, so this is the correct side to err on.

ValidationCalgary

The honest test is spatial

Neighboring cells are nearly identical, so a random split leaks spatial information and overstates performance. Training on the west half and predicting the east estimates performance in unseen territory.

Discrimination survives the spatial test almost untouched. Calibration does not. The eastern half is overpredicted, so transfers are presented as relative risk rather than literal probability.

ResultsCalgary

Where the model errs

False positives adjoin the true positives along the same corridors, misclassifications at the floodplain edge rather than random error. False negatives are rare and isolated. At a nine percent base rate, a flagged cell carries a fourfold concentration of risk even at moderate precision.

Transfer 1 of 2Denver

Denver, the analog

Semi-arid, continental, at the Rocky Mountain foothills, on the South Platte and Cherry Creek. The flood mechanism matches Calgary. Heavy rain on steep terrain drains into valleys where the city sits.

Predicted mass sits near zero, the upper tail is bounded, and high relative risk follows the Platte corridor and the northwest lowlands. The distribution shape matches Calgary.

Transfer 2 of 2Minneapolis

Minneapolis, the contrast

Riverine and continental like Calgary, but flat prairie along the Mississippi. With terrain variation gone, distance is the only feature left with meaningful spread. Predictions saturate in a narrow band along the river trench and the Chain of Lakes.

The model reduces to a river buffer. The reduction is informative. It shows the model encodes foothills-valley hydrology rather than a universal flood signature.

Pipeline

Method, in one sentence

The model predicts flood inundation well where terrain drives hydrology, transfers to a terrain-similar city with its risk ranking intact, and degrades to a river buffer on flat ground. That boundary defines where this class of model can support planning decisions.
